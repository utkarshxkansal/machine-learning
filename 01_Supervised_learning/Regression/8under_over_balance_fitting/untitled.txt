# Underfitting, Overfitting and Balanced Fit

## 1. Underfitting

Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to learn the actual pattern in the data.

### Characteristics:
- High training error
- High testing error
- Poor performance on both training and testing data
- High bias
- Model is too simple

### Example:
Using a straight-line model for data that has a strong curved relationship.

### How to fix:
- Increase model complexity
- Add useful features
- Use polynomial features
- Reduce excessive regularization

---

## 2. Overfitting

Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too well, including noise and random fluctuations.

### Characteristics:
- Very low training error
- High testing error
- Training performance is much better than testing performance
- High variance
- Model is too complex

### Example:
Using a very high-degree polynomial such as degree 15 or 20 for a simple dataset.

### How to fix:
- Reduce model complexity
- Use a lower polynomial degree
- Get more training data
- Use regularization
- Remove unnecessary features
- Use cross-validation

---

## 3. Balanced Fit

A balanced fit occurs when the model is complex enough to learn the important pattern but not so complex that it learns noise.

### Characteristics:
- Low training error
- Low testing error
- Training and testing performance are reasonably close
- Good generalization
- Balanced bias and variance

### Goal:
The model should perform well not only on training data but also on unseen testing data.

---

## Comparison

| Feature | Underfitting | Balanced Fit | Overfitting |
|--------|--------------|--------------|-------------|
| Model complexity | Too low | Appropriate | Too high |
| Training error | High | Low | Very low |
| Testing error | High | Low | High |
| Bias | High | Balanced | Low |
| Variance | Low | Balanced | High |
| Generalization | Poor | Good | Poor |

---

## Polynomial Regression Example

### Degree 1
A degree-1 polynomial is a straight line and may cause underfitting when the data is curved.

### Degree 2 or 3
A degree-2 or degree-3 polynomial may provide a balanced fit if it captures the main pattern without following noise.

### Very High Degree
A very high-degree polynomial may overfit by trying to pass very closely through individual training points.

### Important:
A high polynomial degree does not automatically mean overfitting. Overfitting depends on how the model performs on unseen data.

---

## Easy Way to Remember

Underfitting → Model has not learned enough.

Balanced Fit → Model learned the useful pattern.

Overfitting → Model learned the training data too closely, including noise.

### Main Goal of Machine Learning:

The goal is not to get the lowest training error.

The goal is to build a model that performs well on unseen data.
